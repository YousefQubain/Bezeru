When a Watch Carries a Country

The story behind MING’s Royal Selangor limited edition

Material, memory, place.Editorial image placeholder · no licensed product image in repository

Limited editions are everywhere in watchmaking. Some are made for a retailer, a city, an anniversary or simply to create a little more urgency around a familiar design.

But occasionally, a limited edition feels connected to something larger than its production number.

The MING 37.06 Royal Selangor is one of those watches.

Limited to just 88 pieces, the watch brings together MING, an independent Swiss watch brand known for its modern and sculptural designs, and Royal Selangor, a Malaysian company with more than a century of experience working with pewter.

The result is not a watch that simply places a Malaysian symbol on the dial. Its connection to Malaysia is built into the object itself.

A collaboration with a sense of place

The most immediate detail is the dial.

Instead of using a conventional metal surface, MING and Royal Selangor used hammered pewter. The texture catches the light differently across the dial, giving the watch a surface that feels almost handmade and slightly unpredictable.

That matters because the dial does not look completely uniform. It has depth, movement and small variations. It reminds us that the watch was not created only through computer renders and polished machinery. Someone had to work with the material, shape it and understand how it would respond to light.

The dial is placed inside a 38mm Grade-2 titanium case, creating an interesting contrast between the cool, lightweight modernity of MING and the older craft tradition represented by Royal Selangor.

One side of the collaboration speaks in the language of contemporary watch design. The other speaks through touch, texture and history.

From a small workshop to a global name

Royal Selangor’s story began in 1885 with Yong Koon, a pewtersmith who came to Malaya from Shantou with his tools and knowledge. He settled in Kuala Lumpur and began building a business around pewter craftsmanship.

Today, Royal Selangor remains a family-owned company based in Kuala Lumpur. Its work has expanded far beyond traditional household objects, but the relationship with pewter remains at the centre of its identity.

That history gives the MING collaboration a human dimension. This is not just two companies combining logos. It is a meeting between two different approaches to making things: one rooted in watch design, the other in a Malaysian craft tradition passed through generations.

According to MING, the partnership began through a personal connection. A customer had asked Royal Selangor to reskin a camera, which eventually led MING founder Ming Thein to meet Royal Selangor’s managing director in Kuala Lumpur. A friendship developed, and eventually, so did a watch.

That is a much more compelling origin story than a boardroom decision to create a “regional exclusive.”

Why the production matters

The entire 88-piece production run was assembled in Kuala Lumpur.

This does not mean that every component was produced in Malaysia. The movement comes from Sellita, and the strap was made by Jean Rousseau in Paris. But it does mean that Kuala Lumpur is involved in the physical creation of the watch, not simply its marketing story.

That distinction is important.

Luxury brands often speak about countries through colours, names and limited-edition numbers. Those details can be attractive, but they do not always create a genuine connection. In the MING 37.06 Royal Selangor, Malaysia appears through the material, the partner and the assembly process.

The country is not just mentioned. It is present.

A limited edition that remains a watch

The cultural story is important, but the watch still has to work on the wrist.

The 37.06 measures 38mm across, offers 100 metres of water resistance and has approximately 50 hours of power reserve. Its titanium construction also keeps the watch remarkably light, with the watch head weighing around 35.9 grams.

These details prevent the watch from becoming a decorative object or a commemorative souvenir. It remains practical, wearable and recognisably MING.

That balance is perhaps the most successful part of the collaboration. The watch respects its Malaysian inspiration without becoming overly literal. There is no need for a large flag, a predictable map or an exaggerated national theme. The story is carried by the surface of the dial and by the people who helped make it.

What makes it memorable?

The MING 37.06 Royal Selangor is now sold out, and the 88-piece edition will not be produced again.

But its importance is not really about scarcity.

The more interesting question is what a limited edition should represent. Is it enough for a watch to be rare, or should it also tell us something about where it came from?

In this case, the answer feels clear. The edition works because the story is not printed on top of the watch. It is contained within the watch—in its pewter dial, its Kuala Lumpur assembly and the relationship between two independent makers.

A limited edition does not become meaningful simply because fewer people can own it.

It becomes meaningful when the object could not have existed in quite the same way without the place, the material and the people behind it.
